What changed in Toronto's 2026 budget, in five minutes.

The City spends about $18.9 billion a year to run services. Most of it is the same as last year. This page focuses on the part that changed: which services got more money, which got less, how a $1.34 billion hole was filled, and what it costs you.

How to read "net"

Inflation alone is roughly 2–3% a year, so a small increase can still mean a service is doing the same or less in real terms.

2 · Cuts & savings

How a $1.34 billion hole was filled

Why this matters

Two kinds of tools closed the gap. Savings (cuts, efficiencies, hiring freezes) are usually permanent. Reserve draws spend money the City had saved up. They work for one year, but the same hole comes back the next year unless something else fills it.

      Sources & method

      Where every number comes from

      Figures come from City of Toronto budget documents (Budget Notes, the Mayor's Budget Report, Council decisions), cross-checked against news coverage. Program-level numbers are net operating budgets, meaning the part paid by City revenue after a program's own fares, fees and grants. The 13 programs shown are the large, resident-facing services; the full budget also includes debt payments, corporate accounts and smaller divisions not shown here.

      This is an independent, non-partisan explainer, not an official City product. If you spot an error, please open an issue on the project's repository. Each number on the page links to the document it came from below.
